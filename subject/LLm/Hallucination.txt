14. Hallucination

This is one of the most important concepts in GenAI application engineering, because your job isn’t simply to make an LLM generate text.

First: what is a hallucination?

An LLM hallucination is when the model generates information that sounds plausible but is incorrect, unsupported, or fabricated.


Example:

User: “What is Vixel Digitals’ enterprise plan?”

Suppose the model doesn’t actually have that information.

It might respond:

“The Enterprise plan costs $2,500/month and includes unlimited AI agents.”

Sounds completely reasonable.

But if that plan doesn’t exist, the model hallucinated.




Parametric knowledge → hallucination



User:
"What is the current price of Product X?"

        ↓

LLM
        ↓
Parametric knowledge
        ↓
Model may have old / incomplete / no information
        ↓
Generates plausible answer




1. Missing knowledge

The model simply doesn’t have the required information.

Question
   ↓
Required fact
   ↓
Not available
   ↓
LLM generates plausible continuation
   ↓
Hallucination



2. Outdated knowledge

The model may have learned something during training, but reality changed afterward.





Training:
Product price = $100

Reality:
Product price = $150



3. Ambiguous / insufficient context

Suppose your SDR receives:

“Tell him I’ll take the second package.”

But the conversation doesn’t contain the packages.

The model may infer what “second package” means.

That’s dangerous.





Hallucination is NOT just “wrong information”

There are several forms.

Factual hallucination

Invented fact:

“Stripe was founded in 2008.”

when the generated claim is incorrect.

Fabricated entity

The model invents something that doesn’t exist:

“Vixel Digitals’ AI Enterprise Suite”

when no such product exists.

Fabricated source

The model produces a citation or document that sounds legitimate but doesn’t actually exist.

Unsupported reasoning

The conclusion might sound logical, but the provided evidence doesn’t actually support it.

⸻

Now the important engineering question:

How do we reduce hallucination?

You don’t solve hallucination with one prompt.


The killer rule:

Never assume that because an LLM sounds confident, the information is correct.
